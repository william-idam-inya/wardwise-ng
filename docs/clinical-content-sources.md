# Clinical content sources and review

WardWise NG's offline Clinical Tutor contains three source-linked educational summaries. They are not exhaustive guidelines, clinical decision-support algorithms or instructions to prescribe.

## Jaundice
Source: [Merck Manual Professional Edition — Jaundice](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/hepatic-and-biliary-disorders/approach-to-the-patient-with-liver-disease/jaundice). The guide covers timing of jaundice and dark urine, stool changes, pruritus, pain, relevant exposure/past history, examination, biochemical tests and red flags.

## UTI in children under 16
Source: [NICE NG224 — Urinary tract infection in under 16s](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng224/chapter/Recommendations). NICE published NG224 on 27 July 2022; the recommendations page records a November 2025 link update. It identifies symptoms and signs that increase UTI likelihood, advises that a single feature should not determine testing in isolation, and recommends referral of babies under 3 months with suspected UTI to paediatric specialist care for urgent urine microscopy and culture.

NICE is a UK guideline. The in-app guide expressly asks learners to consider local Nigerian protocols and clinical supervision; it is not presented as a Nigerian national guideline.

## Acute abdominal pain
Source: [Merck Manual Professional Edition — Acute Abdominal Pain](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/gastrointestinal-disorders/acute-abdomen-and-surgical-gastroenterology/acute-abdominal-pain). The guide covers focused pain history, examination sequence, early risk assessment and escalation features.

## Governance
- The in-app review date records when this initial summary was prepared, not independent clinical approval.
- A qualified clinician should review every guide before the material is treated as endorsed teaching content.
- Recheck content when the source changes or local guidelines are updated.
- Keep curated offline content visibly distinct from AI-generated content.


## Differential reasoning and management teaching

Each starter guide now provides a structured differential framework and high-level management principles. These sections ask students to compare possible diagnostic groups with supporting clues and cautions; they do not confirm diagnoses or supply autonomous drug regimens. The paediatric UTI guide explicitly separates NICE guidance from the need to follow local Nigerian/institutional protocols. All differential and management material remains subject to qualified clinician review before being treated as endorsed teaching content.
